RR1 — Retention, submission evidence and recovery for entity drafts
Proposed design; source base 05200e43af13b527ce031b04369e05852862f1d6. Extends candidate05 FD1.11/16, preserves its common revision owner and editing-generation guards. No deletion, deployment or legal conclusion is performed/authorized here.

RR1.1 Source facts and smallest fitting choice
The common instance store (migration0155) rejects all revision DELETE and permits only interval closure. This protects business history and cannot simply be disabled for draft cleanup. Platform erasure-ledger (migration0207, src/lib.rs) already records deletion facts, chains them, exposes head/classify/entries_since, but explicitly has no external witness holder or re-applier. append currently opens its own transaction. Its relation/selector fields are untrusted free text, not replay SQL. ExternalSealSigner verifies against locally pinned keys and delegates signing; the interface is not proof that an independent holder is deployed.
Current deploy/apps/console/base/database.yaml:101 declares retentionPolicy 35d. The inspected prod overlay changes its endpoint, not that policy. Older erasure-ledger comments saying no retention policy are stale relative to this source manifest. No live provider inventory, successful backup pruning, effective overlay readback or restore exercise has been observed. Legal assertions in deployment comments are not adopted as qualified legal conclusions.
Choice: reuse common revision ownership, the existing erasure-fact chain, existing submitted-attempt evidence and platform storage/signing seams. Add only typed retention control, a restricted disposal path, an independently held disposition journal and recovery admission. Soft disposal alone cannot remove retained personal data. Per-draft envelope encryption/key destruction could shorten recoverability but requires key custody, replica/key-backup and query changes; it is not selected without that explicit requirement. A separate draft database or version-control service does not remove the backup/journal problem and is not selected.

RR1.2 Retention units and exact submission evidence
One draft's editable payload history is one logical disposal unit: its complete common-engine revision chain, draft-only metadata/text and draft-owned derived projections. Draft ID and minimal terminal replay metadata survive according to a separately governed metadata policy; identity/Account itself is not a disposal target. Company scopes every operation; a Group request expands into six independent Company jobs when all six are authorized, never mixed-cell DELETE.
Submission already creates an immutable DraftSubmissionAttempt containing exact normalized input, schema/action versions, actor/custody, source/evidence refs and domain command ID (FD1.7). Reuse that artifact as the submitted-input snapshot. Do not copy the whole editable chain into audit, invent a second snapshot engine or treat an input digest alone as retained readable evidence. The business owner explicitly adopts the attempt's required input/evidence under its record retention and disclosure policy, with a result-to-attempt and attempt-to-original-draft-revision lineage link.
A content pin has an immutable owner reference, exact target revision/artifact, purpose/policy version and release authority; it is a governed relationship, not a Boolean editable by a manager. Any active pin on a draft revision retains the whole chain in this first representation. READY/unknown attempts pin their input and block deletion. SUCCEEDED alone does not release the pin: release requires the owner to verify that the separately retained attempt snapshot contains every required submitted input, attachment and context item and that its retention obligation is active. That adoption and pin release share the same Company transaction and guards. If a required item still exists only in the draft, retain its pin. A legal hold on the full writing history remains independent and prevents chain disposal.
After verified adoption, a lineage-only link may retain draft ID/revision ID and an internal integrity reference without retaining private history content. The UI can truthfully show 'original draft history no longer retained; submitted input available' under current rights. Private intermediate notes or abandoned values are not automatically part of submitted evidence. Required notes actually submitted must be included in the adopted snapshot and presented in the owning review flow. Fields must not be omitted just to make disposal eligible.
Normal post-submission reads resolve the domain-adopted attempt snapshot under current entity/property policy, even after source draft history expires. The submitted snapshot is not deletable by draft cleanup. Its own final disposal later uses its domain record policy and owner, outside this limited draft cleanup scope.

RR1.3 Policy, eligibility and user controls
RetentionPolicy is a versioned, Company-scoped governed object defining applicable draft class, start event, duration, legal-hold treatment, metadata duration and accountable policy owner. Candidate05's 30-day private idle expiry / 30-day terminal eligibility remains a proposed operational default, not a statutory rule. Active work drafts do not expire merely because their worker is absent. Expiry/discard first ends editing; it does not withdraw a submitted business request or remove an Account. Restoring a backup does not reset these clocks.
Eligibility requires terminal/discarded/expired input, elapsed applicable window, no unresolved attempt, no content pins or holds, current allowed retention class and a current policy version. A privileged title confers none of these rights. All checkable reasons are typed refs/state/time; human explanations remain evidence, not executable retention predicates. An authorized policy change invalidates unstarted plans and is reevaluated before each deletion batch. Extending a period or adding a hold stops further deletion; shortening a period still requires the same eligibility checks and cannot make active work disposable.
Human-facing actions: discard own eligible unsubmitted draft; inspect authorized retention status; request/apply/release a specifically scoped hold through its owning policy; manually retry/reconcile a failed job. No universal 'force erase' or 'ignore hold' button. Routine eligible cleanup is automatic under the versioned policy. A manual repair path names a typed target and evidence; it does not accept SQL or bypass preserved data protections.
Keep orthogonal facts distinct: payload availability (present/quarantined/removed), active obligations, job progress, external receipt status and backup recoverability. 'Deleted everywhere' is not a single inferred lifecycle enum. A failed archive-pruning check cannot make live content reappear.

RR1.4 Plan and independent journal before effects
prepare_disposal identifies one Company/draft, expected common head/hash and editing context, current retention-policy version and exact dependency/pin/hold generations. Under candidate05's assignment-before-draft/head order it rechecks eligibility, moves the draft to a quarantined terminal control epoch and records a typed manifest/job. No more saves, restores, seals, links/content pins or generic mutations can enter that draft while prepared. Legal holds may still be recorded. Existing authorized result snapshots are unaffected. A repeated prepare with the same command returns the same plan; changed bindings conflict.
Manifest v1 contains fixed owner operation kind 'draft_chain_disposal', Company/draft IDs, exact frozen head/revision bound, schema/profile version, policy/authority refs, attachment/derived-owner cleanup refs and a random disposal ID. It carries no raw draft fields, employee natural keys or user-written predicate. It is sensitive pseudonymous operational data, not anonymous public audit. Manifest scope cannot be expanded by a worker. No arbitrary table selector is accepted.
Before any destructive transaction, an independent disposition journal must durably accept an authenticated INTENT for that exact manifest and return a read-back receipt. Use the platform storage/signing owner; retain immutable signed journal records outside the restored database's failure/administrative domain, plus a conditionally updated authoritative per-Company tip. Conditional object creation/head compare-and-swap and strong readback are required capabilities, not present in the inspected generic S3ObjectStore trait. Extend its concrete storage owner only after provider conformance proof; no assumption that an S3-compatible endpoint provides them correctly. If the holder is unavailable, stay prepared and delete nothing.
The journal sequences INTENT, committed batch/result, known-no-effect cancellation and completion records. A signature binds Company, disposal ID, manifest hash, record kind, predecessor and generation; trusted verification keys come from independently managed pinned configuration, never from restored database records. Key rotation retains prior verification keys for retained records. Journal payloads and latest-tip state must survive DB PITR; a log line, in-cluster copy, or signature with no independently held latest head is insufficient. A tip update referencing an object whose upload/readback did not complete cannot commit. Concurrent tip updates use CAS and bounded retry; orphan unreferenced records are not committed authority.
This journal is required because a post-delete outbox alone can disappear in a restore before being exported. It supplements the existing erasure ledger; it does not claim deletion happened when only INTENT exists. Any unresolved INTENT makes the target unavailable during recovery until its actual outcome is established. It does not authorize recovery to execute arbitrary deletions.

RR1.5 Restricted deletion and bounded batches
Deletion remains owned by the common revision owner. Design a fixed SQL operation callable only by a dedicated retention executor, with a NOLOGIN non-superuser non-BYPASSRLS function owner, pinned safe search_path, Company checks and no runtime role membership/SET ROLE path. The ordinary application role gets no general DELETE privilege and no execution right for the destructive function. The executor must independently validate the registered draft retention class, manifest, policy and obligations; a forged queue row from the app is not authority. Registry ownership makes this draft classification immutable to runtime clients. Projected business types and their chains are excluded by positive classification and owner checks.
Migration0155's unconditional guard must be replaced prospectively by a narrower guard that retains rejection for ordinary operations and business revisions, and permits only the restricted retention function's exact frozen manifest scope. A client-set GUC or caller-provided plan ID is not sufficient proof. Trigger/function ownership, ACLs, plan custody and fixed queries must be validated together under real runtime/executor roles. Do not disable triggers, loosen tests or use a superuser to make the path work. This is a required reviewed migration, not SQL implemented by this evidence.
One draft is the logical deletion unit, but a large chain is processed in bounded physical batches. Initial validation assumptions: at most 500 revision rows and 4 MiB of payload per batch, one job per Company and two jobs total, two-second statement timeout with bounded retry. A single row is at most candidate05's 64 KiB payload bound. Plans with incompatible historic oversize rows stop for a specifically reviewed handling path; they are never silently truncated. Timeouts roll back the entire batch. Bytes/rows bounds do not prove I/O latency; validate index/FK/WAL costs in the later runtime proof.
Before each batch acquire the same assignment/control/head fence and current policy/obligation guards, check journal INTENT receipt, manifest identity, expected job checkpoint and explicitly executable local plan phase. CANCEL_PENDING/CANCELLED and recovery-unknown plans reject every destructive path; an old INTENT receipt alone never overrides the cancellation fence. Delete the next fixed ordered subset of revision payloads; write exact row count/ID-bound batch receipt, advance checkpoint, and append the existing erasure ledger's live-deletion facts in the SAME Company transaction. Extend erasure-ledger with a transaction-participating owner function; its current pool-level append transaction is not sufficient. Never record all planned rows as erased before the batch commits. Export each committed batch receipt through the journal before advancing to the next batch; response loss reconciles the same batch identity. Unknown commit blocks progress; no fresh batch ID guesses.
While any batch has committed, the object stays quarantined and cannot be returned as a shortened valid history. The control head pointer/reader contract must tolerate disposal progress without dangling ordinary reads; no live reader may follow a deleted revision. A legal hold racing a batch serializes on the same fence: hold first => no delete; batch first => record what was removed, accept a hold on remaining material and pause subsequent batches. Remaining fragments are accessible only through a separately authorized forensic recovery path, not a reconstructed 'complete' draft. Releasing the hold requires current authority and a fresh eligibility check, not silent resumption under the old policy. Already deleted bytes cannot be promised back.
Pins arriving after preparation fail with an authorized unavailable/disposing outcome rather than resurrecting dependencies. Before any deletion, cancellation is a three-step protocol. First, under the same batch/assignment/control guards, prove zero committed batches and no unresolved effect, then COMMIT a local CANCEL_PENDING tombstone with a stable cancellation command ID. This durable phase rejects every destructive path and cannot roll back to PREPARED on retry. Second, outside that transaction, append/read back the exact terminal journal cancellation. A failure or lost response stays fenced and reconciles that same cancellation identity; no automatic unquarantine. Third, finalize local CANCELLED under the guards and advance the control epoch. Release quarantine only when no effect and external cancellation are proven and current policy permits access. Cancellation does not reset retention clocks, reverse expiry or automatically make a terminal draft editable. A separately permitted resume uses a fresh editing epoch. If the first batch wins the race, cancellation cannot claim no effect or publish CANCELLED; it follows the partial-removal/hold path. After any committed deletion, cancellation cannot restore the draft. Keep it quarantined until complete or held/repair-resolved.


RR1.6 Copies, finalization and durable receipts
Inventory every payload-bearing surface before activation: common revisions, draft-only title/reason/metadata, pending input snapshot copies not adopted by a domain, save receipts, audit snapshots, links/search projections, caches, attachments/versions, replicas, exports and backups. A runtime route that still copies raw draft attributes into an immutable broad audit/receipt makes safe cleanup incomplete; fix/minimize that path before accepting real drafts. Do not purge business receipts to hide an accidental leak.
Ordinary save receipts retain minimal command/actor binding and version result, not complete payload. Typed tombstones prevent any old save/execute from recreating removed intent. Retention/replay evidence can itself identify a person and remains policy-governed; no infinite metadata retention is asserted. Draft metadata retention must cover the maximum accepted command replay horizon, all usable backup/recovery points and unresolved outcomes. Once metadata disposal is allowed, unknown draft IDs still cannot be recreated by update/execute; only an explicit fresh start with a new random ID is possible.
Release attachment references through the storage owner, preserving any domain-adopted or other active references and holds. Do not execute a bucket/key supplied by a client as a deletion target. Exact storage object/version IDs and derivative refs come from the frozen owner manifest. In a versioned bucket, deleting a key may only add a delete marker; verify removal/retention status of every relevant retained version. Failure or object-lock retention remains visible as pending copy cleanup, never proof of destruction. Already issued downloaded files or third-party copies are not remotely erased by this mechanism; managed exports need their own tracked custody/retention action.
Once all live draft payload batches and required owned projections are gone, keep a minimal tombstone and immutable final live-removal receipt referencing actual batch/erasure-ledger checkpoints. Journal export/readback must complete before the operator status claims externally recorded live removal. A separate copy-inventory status tracks remaining archive/replica/export material. Search caches are not restored as truth; rebuild authorized projections after cleanup. Current reads omit disposed content from all routes even during lagging replica/cache cleanup; admission tokens/generations and invalidation enforce this, not eventual deletion alone.

RR1.7 Recovery admission and unknown outcomes
Restore first into an isolated, non-serving environment. Keep HTTP/SSR, search, exports, subscriptions, schedules and effect workers disabled. Fence the former writer/deployment generation before any restored writer can run; an old worker must not resume with a stale lease. Independent operations control holds the active recovery generation and fresh journal tip; they are not restored from the same backup. Exact provider/runtime fencing and permissions are release prerequisites, not assumed existing Kubernetes behavior.
Fetch and verify the current independent journal tip and chain (including Company's removal/tombstone history), compare it with the restored DB erasure ledger using existing classify plus full needed chain checks. A digest witness can detect missing history but cannot supply missing deletion scope. Replay uses the retained typed manifests and committed results, never SQL built from erasure_ledger.erased_relation/erased_selector. Legacy entries lacking a trusted typed scope block affected admission pending manual owner mapping and approval; do not infer scope from free text.
Before replay or admission, an externally confirmed CANCELLED disposition makes every stale restored PREPARED/INTENT-bearing local plan non-executable through a durable terminal cancellation guard. If contradictory committed deletion evidence exists, quarantine for integrity investigation; do not prefer either record silently. A locally restored CANCEL_PENDING without external confirmation remains fenced until reconciliation; it cannot restart deletion.
For each externally confirmed committed batch, the recovery-only common owner operation reapplies precisely that frozen manifest/batch scope idempotently to the restored target, records an application receipt for this recovery generation, and preserves the original deletion provenance. It checks Company/type/ID bounds and retained artifacts, not whether an old restored policy would have approved deletion. The already-confirmed deletion must not be undone by stale policy or a stale restored pin. If a supposedly confirmed scope conflicts with independently current required evidence/hold records or checksum identities, quarantine and investigate; never broadly delete around the mismatch.
An INTENT with no proven final outcome is not a confirmed delete or confirmed cancellation. Quarantine the named draft and dependent views/effect paths; recover the originating DB transaction/ledger/WAL evidence or obtain a governed resolution from the responsible owner using that evidence. A missing receipt in the restored DB proves nothing. If source evidence cannot establish the outcome, keep that target blocked; do not invent success, delete everything or cancel based on timeout. Unaffected Company data may be admitted only when dependency closure and current authority are proven; otherwise the Company stays quarantined. Partial admission is explicit, not a silent skip.
Recover current policies, grants, Account/session revocations, assignment epochs and business command/attempt receipts before admission. Restoring old copies of those is not current authority. Establish their current state from verified current recovery sources or explicit governed re-establishment while isolated; invalidate all pre-recovery sessions/tokens and do not auto-replay queued business Actions. If a newer business effect may have occurred beyond the recoverable DB point, reconcile its domain receipt/effect before enabling that target. The disposal journal is not a substitute for general business recovery evidence.
Admission requires exact restored image/schema IDs, verified journal tip and applied result watermark, resolved or explicitly isolated unknown targets, current authority state, owner-consistent submission evidence, rebuilt projections, new serving generation and an authorized operations receipt. Changes to the independent tip during reconciliation require another bounded catch-up before admission. Empty local ledger or a healthy database process is not admission proof.

RR1.8 Backup horizon, integrity and truthful claims
The manifest's 35d is a configured recovery window, not an exact deadline for physical erasure. Base-backup dependencies, WAL needed to make the oldest retained backup recoverable, prune failures, replicas and object versions may retain older data. Inventory actual backup objects and all dependencies; record oldest usable recovery point, last successful prune, provider version/lock status and outstanding copies. Do not compute 'destroyed_at = deleted_at + 35d'. A disabled or failed cleanup job cannot be masked by a passed date.
Never surgically edit shared WAL/base-backup files to remove one person's rows; it would invalidate recovery for the whole Company/cluster. Retain backups according to separately approved operations policy and apply the mandatory restoration scrub before service. If requirements demand non-reconstructability before the archive horizon, this physical-cleanup design is insufficient: evaluate envelope-key destruction with separate key backups/custody, rather than claim quarantine is cryptographic erasure.
Submitted snapshots and retained revision chains use the common engine's integrity data; journal receipts reuse external signer trust boundaries. Periodic independently held checkpoints of relevant revision/attempt roots and erasure/journal positions can detect rollback/tampering through that checkpoint; a signature stored only in the restored database cannot detect removal of the entire suffix. Dirty/uncheckpointed suffixes have explicitly weaker recovery assurance. An independently held hash does not reconstruct lost plaintext and does not make a submitted approval portable authority.
For first activation, prove a backup/restore drill against known retained submitted evidence and disposed draft fixtures, plus external journal unavailability/fork/stale-tip/key-rotation cases. Document actual RPO/RTO and largest measured dataset; no new numerical service promise is inferred from 2,000 people or the 35d setting. Failure to verify a retained snapshot blocks its restoration/use; do not recompute a matching chain over corrupted data and call it repaired. Repair replaces from a verified source with provenance, or records unrecoverable loss through an authorized owner path.

RR1.9 Migration and rollback contract
Migration admission is additive control/manifest/batch/tombstone structures, typed retention classification and same-tx erasure participation; then restricted trigger/function changes proven against original invariants. Preserve old business rows, hash chains, Account references, effective dates and single writers. Inventory existing draft-like objects and raw audit/receipt payload copies before classification; no automatic conversion of all objects in generic Draft state into disposable private drafts. Existing business/private misclassification fails closed for activation.
Rollout order: validated policy and copy inventory -> common-owner/ACL tests -> independent journal and verification readback -> synthetic prepare/cancel -> non-sensitive disposal fixture and isolated restore drill -> narrowly admitted real draft class only under later approved release. Physical deletion is irreversible; ordinary rollback stops new preparation/deletion but keeps pending-job reconciliation, quarantine/tombstone readers, journal access and restore scrubbing. Never revert to the old trigger/reader combination mid-job or serve a restored pre-deletion backup as rollback. Failed migration restores original behavior only before any destructive job; after that use a forward-compatible fix while fenced.
Remaining provider configuration, secrets, deployed-state evidence, migrations/runtime tests, qualified retention periods, off-device custody and production exposure require their separate approvals/proofs. This candidate specifies what they must prove; it is not a claim that any have run. B03/B04/B07/B08 and overall design/test SHA approvals remain open. The stop for this packet is independently reviewed retention/recovery behavior and non-executable oracles, not a production-ready verdict.
